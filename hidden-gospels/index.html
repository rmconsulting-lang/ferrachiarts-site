Nag Hammadi Library study guide

Hidden Gospels is a beginner’s companion to the books found in a buried jar in Egypt in 1945, read over forty short days. The ancient texts themselves are not included.

Over forty days the guide walks through every writing in the thirteen Nag Hammadi codices, including the Gospel of Thomas, the Gospel of Philip, the Gospel of Truth, the Secret Book of John, and the Apocalypse of Adam. Each day says where to find the text (codex and tractate, or saying numbers for Thomas), how hard it is, what it says in plain English, how it compares with the Bible (King James Version), and three questions to think about. No Greek, Coptic, or theology background is assumed.

The plan is in six parts: the hidden gospels (days 1–9), the soul and wisdom (days 10–14), the Secret Book of John (days 15–21), Valentinus and his school (days 22–27), apostles and revelations (days 28–32), and Seth and the heavenly ascent (days 33–40). Later pages set Enoch material — the Watchers, Seth, Melchizedek, and the heavenly journeys — beside the Nag Hammadi books, for readers who also use Walking with Enoch.

The texts are separate

This guide does not include the Nag Hammadi writings. Modern English translations are under copyright, so Hidden Gospels is a companion you read alongside an edition you already have. Any of these works:

  • The Nag Hammadi Library in English, edited by James M. Robinson
  • The Nag Hammadi Scriptures, edited by Marvin Meyer
  • The Gnostic Society Library at gnosis.org

Days are numbered by codex and tractate, so the same plan fits those editions. Bible quotations in the guide are from the King James Version. The summaries, questions, notes, and charts were drafted with the help of AI tools, then checked and edited by Ferrachi Arts.
